A register the
morning shift can act on

Friday’s screening kit came back in pieces. I rebuilt the register so Saturday’s shift can see what is still out, who has it, and close the loan on the right unit without reading Friday’s notes.

Play the shift

Walked through the full scenario: 3 units issued at 3:45 pm, 2 back at 7:30 pm, 1 adapter out overnight.

The moment I would change

Saturday, 9 am. Leela walks in with an adapter.

The job is to find what was outstanding from Friday and close the right loan. The original register made that a guessing game.

Before

L-404 · AD-12RQ-214 · Kabir Sen · Due Fri 8 pm
Record return
  • No model or bin. AD-12 and AD-15 both live in bin C4, so a guess can close the loan on the wrong unit.
  • No sign it is late. Friday’s adapter looks the same on Saturday morning.
  • The handover lives elsewhere. “Leela has the adapter” sits in the Desk book, a different page from the slip it describes.

What it already gets right

  • Repair units never appear. PR-06 is in the workshop, so it is not offered at pickup.
  • Quantities are enforced. Giving the Film Society a second projector is refused because the approved one is already out.
  • One slip per physical unit. A partial return can be recorded exactly.

A tired person at a 3:45 pm counter cannot create a bad slip. I kept every one of these guardrails.

The change

Organised around the kit, closed by the tag

The kit is the unit of work

Slips sit under their request with a header like “3 of 3 approved units issued · 1 still out”, so a partial return reads at a glance.

Overdue comes from the desk clock

Every due time is read against desk time. Friday’s adapter shows “Overdue by 13 h” on Saturday, and that kit moves to the top.

The handover lives on the slip

Held by, Expected back and a short note are saved on the slip itself, and the Desk book opens with a summary of what is still out.

The tag closes the slip

Record return asks for the asset tag on the unit in hand and warns that AD-15 shares bin C4. A different tag keeps the slip open and says where that unit belongs.

The handover belongs on the slip it describes, not on a page the next shift has to go looking for.

What I would watch at a real counter

Does the morning shift close the slip without opening the Desk book?

If they still scroll through handover notes to find out who has what, the handover on the slip is not doing its job.

Second signal

Count returns where the tag in hand does not match the slip. Before the change nothing caught them. Now each one appears as a “Different tag”, and none should end with a closed slip for the wrong unit.

What would make me drop it

If staff tap through the tag check without looking, it is friction without safety. I would watch for checks confirmed in under a second and move to scanning the tag instead.

Questions a reviewer might ask

FAQ

Why group by request instead of listing slips?

Desk staff talk about the Film Society’s kit, not slip 404. Grouping shows a partial return at a glance and still keeps one slip per physical unit, which the original register got right.

Why not add a barcode scanner?

A scanner is the stronger fix for wrong unit returns, and the tag check is shaped so a scan can replace the tap later. It needs hardware and a label on every unit. The check works with what the desk has today.

Why structured fields instead of a free text note?

A free text note only works if people write good ones. Held by and Expected back are the two facts the morning shift needs, so they are pickers. A short free text line stays for anything else.

What happens if the wrong adapter comes back?

Different tag keeps the slip open and explains each case. If AD-15 is not out on any slip, it goes back to bin C4. A unit on another slip is returned on that slip. Anything else is held at the desk with a handover note.

How is overdue worked out?

The desk clock and every return time are read as minutes since Friday midnight and compared directly. There is no table of special cases to keep up to date.

What did you leave alone?

Per unit slips, the issue guardrails, the shift timeline, and the Desk book for messages that are not about one slip.
